What's actually on it

Anatomy with real class strings · token usage · field-by-field validation and error copy · tab order · 11 states · build order.

Written second, on purpose: the PRD decides whether, the screen spec decides what.

Every acceptance criterion names something you can run — node scripts/flow-test.mjs, node scripts/sb-smoke.mjs. "Feels fast" and "is intuitive" are not criteria.

The rule that keeps a spec honest

It had to name its own weak sections

The skill requires naming what's thin, instead of handing over something polished and vague. Four went in:

  • 1The workaround is unknown. "What do agents do today when the customer isn't in the system" has no answer — no agent research exists. Filed as an open question and labelled the weakest part of the document, rather than filled with a plausible invention.
  • 2No success measure is possible here. No backend, no telemetry, no baseline. The PRD says so and lists what would be instrumented later — instead of inventing a percentage.
  • 3Risk 1 is unbudgeted work. A brand-new customer's 360 has no products, bills or history — and those three cards were never designed empty. A direct consequence of the approved destination, landing on the designer.
  • 4The duplicate design is inferred, not observed. Stated in the research, the deck, and again here.

Stage 03 · takeaway

Research decides what. The codebase decides whether.

Three of this spec's decisions changed after reading real files: a 404 that would have shipped, a component that turned out to already exist, and a search field that silently discards what the agent types.

A PRD written from the research alone would have looked just as convincing — and been wrong three times.

Next: the designer takes it. Four new files, zero new tokens — and three bugs that only appear once something is actually running.
